# Star, Idaho — Research Notes

Researched 2026-08-08. Sources logged in `sources.md`.

## Verified facts

- Ada County. Sits on the **north bank of the Boise River**, west of Eagle, east of Middleton.
- Growth is the defining fact. City of Star's own 2025 demographic report was commissioned
  because of the pace. Aggregator figures put 2026 population around 22,500 and roughly a
  doubling since the 2020 census — directionally consistent with city and news reporting,
  but **the aggregator number itself is weak sourcing**. Copy should describe the *pattern*
  (roughly doubled since 2020) rather than assert a precise headcount.
- **SH-16** runs north–south on Star's eastern edge. ITD is widening SH-16 between SH-44 and
  Beacon Light Road to four lanes with a median barrier, plus an overpass at Floating Feather
  Road. Public meeting anticipated summer 2026. (ITD, primary.)
- **SH-44 (State Street)** is the main east–west route through town toward Eagle and Boise.
  Mayor Chadwick has publicly described standing rush-hour traffic on SH-44. (KTVB.)
- City budget has lagged the growth curve — reported directly by KTVB. Honest tradeoff.

## What this actually means on the ground

Star reads as a town whose street grid and services were built for a much smaller place and
are being retrofitted in real time. The historic core near State Street is small; nearly
everything else is subdivision built in the last decade or under construction now.

The Boise River is the southern boundary and the reason the town exists where it does, but
river frontage is not the everyday experience for most Star buyers the way foothill access is
in Eagle. Worth being accurate about.

## Differentiation vs neighbours

- **vs Eagle:** Eagle has the foothills, an established downtown, and a comprehensive plan
  actively trying to *restrain* development. Star has flatter ground, newer stock, and a
  government trying to *catch up* to development. Different postures entirely.
- **vs Middleton:** Star is Ada County; Middleton is Canyon County. That changes taxing
  district, school district, and highway district. Middleton retains more working farmland.
- **vs Meridian:** Meridian's growth is largely built out and now infilling around big
  commercial nodes. Star's is still edge expansion into former farm ground.

## Tradeoffs to state plainly

- SH-44 congestion at commute hours is real and acknowledged by the mayor.
- Services and roads are catching up rather than leading.
- "25 minutes to Boise" is optimistic at peak; off-peak it's reasonable.

## Copy angle

The honest story is a town in the middle of a transition it did not fully control, where the
road network is the constraint and SH-16 is the variable that changes the math. Lead on
infrastructure and geography, not on "small-town charm."

Working headline direction: something about the highway rewriting the commute, or about
a town being rebuilt around roads that arrived after the houses.

## Do not use

- "Small-town charm," "flocking," "room to grow."
- Any framing about who a place is "great for" (Fair Housing).
- The existing page's "same top-rated schools as Eagle" claim — unverified and comparative.
