# Middleton, Idaho — Research Notes

Researched 2026-08-08. Sources in `sources.md`.

## Verified facts

- **Canyon County**, not Ada. Separate school district (Middleton School District),
  separate county services, different highway district than Star/Eagle/Meridian.
- Sits west of Star along the Boise River corridor, north of Caldwell.
- **School capacity is the defining local issue.** Heights Elementary around 145% of
  capacity, Mill Creek around 114%. Roughly 22 full classrooms housed in portables
  across the two schools.
- **VERIFIED / CORRECTED 2026-08-08:** the ~$19.9M bond did **not** pass. It went to
  voters May 20, 2025 and failed. Idaho requires a **66.6% supermajority** for school
  bonds. Four earlier bond elections between 2018 and 2022 also failed to clear that
  bar — so five attempts in total. The 2022 ask was $54M for multiple projects; the
  2025 version was cut ~79% to elementary-only, at about $34 per $100,000 of taxable
  value, sited on Kingbury Road near a growing subdivision.
  - After the failure the superintendent proposed **redrawing elementary boundaries**;
    even perfectly balanced, each school would sit near 107% of capacity.
  - No confirmed 2026 election found. **Do not assert anything about a current or
    upcoming vote** — describe the pattern through May 2025 only.
  - Earlier draft of these notes said a bond was "proposed." That was wrong and would
    have published a stale claim.
- Local economy leans agricultural — food processing, agricultural equipment, retail
  and services serving farm operations.
- Idaho Public Charter School Commission approved the **Idaho AgriTech Academy**, the
  first agriculture-focused charter school in the state, in Canyon County. Reinforces
  that agriculture here is a live industry, not just scenery.
- Regional pattern: Treasure Valley growth is pushing households west out of Ada County
  into Canyon County. Middleton is directly in that path.

## What this means on the ground

Middleton is the clearest remaining example in the near valley of a town where working
agriculture and subdivision development sit immediately adjacent. Irrigation
infrastructure, field edges, and open sightlines persist right up against new rooftops.

That is genuinely different from Star, where most farm ground inside the city has already
converted.

## Differentiation vs neighbours

- **vs Star:** county line matters — different taxing and school districts. Middleton has
  more intact farmland and a smaller share of post-2015 housing.
- **vs Caldwell/Nampa:** Middleton is smaller and less industrial, more oriented to the
  river corridor.
- **vs Kuna:** both agricultural in feel, but Kuna's open space is high desert canyon
  country; Middleton's is irrigated valley farmland. Very different landscapes.

## Tradeoffs to state plainly

- Schools are over capacity and the district has gone to voters about it.
- Commute to Boise is genuinely longer than from Star or Eagle.
- Growth pressure from Ada County is arriving whether or not the town is ready.

## Copy angle

Lead with the agricultural landscape as an active economy rather than a backdrop, and be
straight about school capacity. Honesty here is a differentiator — nobody else says it.

## Do not use

- "Small-town charm with easy access to city amenities" (the exact phrasing the client
  flagged as generic).
- Any "great for families" framing — Fair Housing.
